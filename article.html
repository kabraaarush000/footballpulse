Why Argentina’s High Press Could Unravel at Altitude in Mexico City

By FootballPulse Analysis • 13 May 2026 • 8 min read

Argentina remain one of the favourites for the FIFA World Cup 2026, but concerns are growing over how their aggressive pressing system will perform in high-altitude venues across Mexico.

The Altitude Problem

Mexico City sits over 2,200 meters above sea level. Teams relying on intense pressing often struggle physically in these conditions due to lower oxygen availability and faster fatigue accumulation.

Lionel Scaloni’s side depends heavily on coordinated pressing triggers, compact midfield spacing, and relentless transitions. Maintaining that intensity for 90 minutes becomes significantly harder at altitude.

“High pressing at altitude is one of the toughest tactical challenges in football.”

Midfield Concerns

Argentina’s midfield looked vulnerable against physically dominant teams throughout 2024 qualifying campaigns. Opponents bypassed the first press more frequently when Argentina’s tempo dropped.

If that trend continues in North America, Scaloni may need tactical adjustments including a deeper block or more conservative pressing.

Could Tactical Flexibility Save Them?

Argentina still possess world-class technical quality and tournament experience. Tactical flexibility rather than constant pressing may be the key to another deep World Cup run.

Rotational pressing systems and better game-state management could help preserve energy levels in difficult environmental conditions.
